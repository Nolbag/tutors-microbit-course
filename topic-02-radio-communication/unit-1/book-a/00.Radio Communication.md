# Radio Communication

Getting micro:bits talking

## What is it?

Micro:bits can send messages to each other wirelessly using their built-in radio. That's what makes remote controls possible, and it's how your joystick will drive your car.

We use **radio groups** so that only certain micro:bits listen to each other. It's like pairing wireless headphones to your phone: your music doesn't play in the headphones of the person sitting next to you. Micro:bits only hear messages sent on **their own group number** (0 to 255).

**EVERY TEAM MUST USE A UNIQUE RADIO GROUP NUMBER.** If two teams pick the same number, your remote will drive their car too!

## Radio blocks

These are in the **Radio** (pink) section:

| Block | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| **radio set group 1** | Picks the group number. Put this in **on start**. Sender and receiver **must match**. |
| **radio send string "F"** | Sends a piece of text |
| **radio send number 0** | Sends a number |
| **on radio received receivedString** | An **event** that runs when text arrives. `receivedString` is a variable holding the message. |
| **on radio received receivedNumber** | Same, but for numbers |

## Senders and receivers

- The **sender** sends a message when something happens, e.g. a button press.
- The **receiver** waits for messages and decides what to do with them, usually with **if / else if**.

In the example below, the sender sends a string or a number depending on which button is pressed. The receiver shows whatever it gets on its LEDs.

![Sender_and_Receiver](img/sender-receiver.png)

> **Agreeing on a code.** The sender and receiver have to agree on what each message means. For your car, the team uses this code:
>
> | Message | Meaning |
> |---------|---------|
> | `"F"` | Forward |
> | `"B"` | Backwards (reverse) |
> | `"L"` | Turn left |
> | `"R"` | Turn right |
> | `"stop"` | Stop |
