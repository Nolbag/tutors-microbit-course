# Meet the micro:bit

Before writing any code, take a look at your micro:bit. Everything you program will use these parts.

## Front

| Part | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| **LED screen** | 25 red lights in a 5 x 5 grid. You can show icons, numbers, words and animations. It can also measure light level. |
| **Button A and Button B** | Inputs. You'll use these all the time, e.g. "when button A is pressed, do something". |
| **Touch logo** (gold logo at the top) | Works like a third button you touch instead of press. *(micro:bit V2 only)* |
| **Pins** (gold strip at the bottom) | Connect the micro:bit to other electronics, like the motors in your car. |

## Back

| Part | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| **Processor** | The "brain" that runs your code. It also contains a temperature sensor. |
| **Accelerometer** | Senses movement: shaking, tilting and which way up it is. |
| **Compass** | Senses which direction the micro:bit is facing. |
| **Radio and Bluetooth antenna** | Lets micro:bits talk to each other wirelessly. This is how your remote will control your car! |
| **Speaker and microphone** | Plays sounds and hears noise levels. *(micro:bit V2 only)* |
| **USB socket** | Connects to your laptop to load code and power the micro:bit. |
| **Battery socket** | Runs the micro:bit without a laptop. |

> **Inputs and outputs.** An *input* is information going **into** the micro:bit (a button press, a tilt, a radio message). An *output* is something the micro:bit **does** (lights up LEDs, plays a sound, turns a motor). Almost every program you write takes an input and decides on an output.

## Quick check

Look at your micro:bit and find:
1. Button A and button B
2. The USB socket
3. The radio antenna (hint: it's on the back, labelled "BLE antenna")
4. Does yours have a gold touch logo? If so, it's a **V2**.
